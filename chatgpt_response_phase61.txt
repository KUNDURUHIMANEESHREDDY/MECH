The scientific objective

Phase 61 should answer:

Can MECH maintain multiple competing theories across generations, actively test them against one another, discover genuinely new branches when residuals demand them, and avoid collapsing prematurely onto the first plausible explanation?

That is a much harder problem than Phase 60.

The critical experiment for Phase 61

I would make one benchmark deliberately adversarial:

Start with three plausible theories
T1 — simple transportability law
T2 — substrate-conditioned law
T3 — new primitive / mechanism

Give MECH an open-world task where:

all three explain existing observations,

their predictions diverge on several interventions,

one is simplest,

one is most flexible,

one is initially least probable but potentially correct.

Then let the tournament run without a manually specified correct answer.

MECH should:

generate the prediction matrix;

choose the most discriminating experiment;

execute it;

update posteriors;

eliminate or retain theories;

generate a new branch if all fail;

repeat until a stopping criterion is met.

Phase 61 success criteria

I would define a preregistered gate such as:

PHASE 61 TOURNAMENT CERTIFICATE


✓ ≥90% correct theory retention
✓ ≥90% discriminating-experiment selection
✓ ≤5% premature theory collapse
✓ ≤5% false theory elimination
✓ ≥90% abstention when population is underdetermined
✓ ≥90% successful recovery of missing theory after population failure
✓ no deletion of falsified theory lineage
✓ complexity penalty enforced
✓ ≥2 generations of theory evolution
✓ at least one branch eliminated by prospective evidence
✓ at least one branch survives on fresh holdouts

The two-generation requirement matters. Otherwise Phase 61 is just Phase 60 with multiple candidates.
