But Phase 61 has also reached an important architectural ceiling:

The tournament is still a bounded evaluation loop.

It can evolve theories inside a tournament, but Phase 62 should make the system continuously decide what scientific problem to investigate next, whether its current theory population is adequate, when new primitives are needed, and when to stop or abstain.

The biggest conceptual change

Phase 61:

MECH evolves theories
	​


Phase 62:

MECH evolves the scientific process itself
	​


It no longer waits for a human to say:

“Now test another model.”

It determines:

“Given everything I currently know, this is the most valuable unresolved scientific question, this is the experiment that best distinguishes the possibilities, this is my prediction, and this is when I should stop claiming more than the evidence supports.”

That is the point where your “autonomous scientist” architecture becomes a continuous system rather than a sequence of increasingly sophisticated benchmarks.

And I would keep one hard rule from all previous phases:

The system must be allowed to conclude: “I don’t know what to investigate next.”
	​


That is arguably the final safeguard needed before letting the loop run continuously.
